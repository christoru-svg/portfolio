Training project · Jan – Apr 2022

Lexis 360 Intelligence helping users find their bearings again

How do you guide legal professionals through a brand-new version of their legal research tool when 88.6% of them chose to skip the guided tour?

Client
Lexis Nexis
Role
Product designer & Scrum Master
Duration
4 months
Status
V2 prototype shipped
01 · Context

A confusing new version, an ignored onboarding

Lexis Nexis, a long-established legal publisher, created Lexis 360, a digital platform for legal professionals that brings together more than 16 million pieces of content (codes, articles, case law) with a specialized search engine. The platform evolved into a new version, Lexis 360 Intelligence, with an entirely new interface and new features.

An onboarding flow and a guided tour had been built to help users discover what was new. This project was carried out as a collaboration between Lexis Nexis and the DPN UX 2022 program, from January to April 2022, using Figma, Miro, Notion, Google Meet and Google Sheets.

Key takeaway: 88.6% of users chose to skip the guided tour, which they saw as a waste of time.

02 · Problem

How do we help users get the most out of the tool?

Users are legal professionals (lawyers, in-house counsel, judges, chartered accountants, notaries) with very different levels of proficiency with the tool, which made one-size-fits-all guidance a poor fit.

  • An onboarding and guided tour skipped by the vast majority (88.6% of users)
  • User profiles with highly varied needs and levels of proficiency
  • The added value of the new version was hard to see compared to the old one
03 · Solution

A co-designed prototype, across two iteration loops

Over the four months, two iteration loops and a first prototype tested with users helped refine the solution into a V2, meant to be tested directly by Lexis Nexis on its platform.

  • Simplify how content is presented to capture and guide the user's attention
  • A flow built around an “aha moment,” so the tool's added value is immediately clear
  • Contextual help throughout the experience, instead of a standalone onboarding at the very start
  • An onboarding that lets users set preferences, to tailor the content shown to each profile
04 · Approach

A user-centered design process, in 4 phases

The action plan was defined with the client from the outset, following a classic user-centered design process.

Phase 1 · Audit and analysis

Understand what exists before designing

Audit and analysis of the existing product, competitive analysis and industry best practices.

Phase 2 · User research

Combine qualitative and quantitative research

Qualitative interviews, an online quantitative survey, secondary research and a competitive benchmark. Four key insights emerged: access to information was too complex, help wasn't contextual, the added value over the old version wasn't perceived, and content didn't match user profiles.

Phase 3 · Ideation and co-creation

Generate the first ideas to test

Several co-creation workshops facilitated with the team and with the client, which produced a first version (V1) of the prototype.

Phase 4 · Prototyping and testing

Validate, then iterate with real users

Eye-tracking tests with 3 users at the Saint-Marcel campus usability lab, complemented by 3 remote tests over Google Meet. The feedback and pain points we gathered fed a first iteration toward the V2.

Leadership

Scrum Master and product designer, a dual role

Beyond design, I served as Scrum Master on this project: setting the team's working rhythm and smoothing collaboration with the client.

Wearing both hats kept the action plan clear over four months, balancing the constraints of the DPN UX program with Lexis Nexis's real expectations.

05 · Results

A user-validated prototype, ready for V2

The project wrapped up with a V2 prototype refined from user feedback, meant to be tested directly by Lexis Nexis on its own platform: the natural next step for work done in a training program rather than in production.
