Case study · 2024

myOodriveSign signing on the go, without wasting time

Giving the busiest signers (CEOs and executives) a space to review and sign their documents in moments from their phone, when no signer space had existed until then.

Client
Oodrive Sign
Role
Lead designer
Duration
Aug – Nov 2024
Status
Shipped
01 · Context

No dedicated signer space, access through email only

Until this project, Oodrive Sign had no real signer space: signers could only reach the documents awaiting their signature through notification emails, with the option to sign several at once from that single entry point.

The need to go further came straight from the field. Sales teams and CSMs were in touch with a clearly identified user profile: CEO-type signers and executives, in constant demand, who needed to move fast and sign on the go rather than from a computer.

Key takeaway: the trigger wasn't an internal product request, but a need raised directly by sales and CSMs working with customers.

02 · Problem

Demanding signers, with no space designed for how they work

The target profile has little time and often signs between meetings, from their phone: a use case the existing product, built for computer access via email, didn't cover.

  • No dedicated signer space: access to documents depended entirely on notification emails
  • An experience designed until then for computers, while the target audience (CEOs, executives) needs to sign on the go
  • A user profile with little availability for traditional discovery interviews
03 · Solution

myOodriveSign, a mobile signer space built for speed

The answer was to create Oodrive Sign's very first signer space as a mobile app, independent of the notification email and designed from the start for quick use between meetings.

  • Created the very first signer space, independent of the notification email
  • A mobile app built for speed: review and sign several documents in moments
  • A deliberately short flow, suited to use between meetings rather than a dedicated block of time

The mobile flow, from the list to the signature. Tap the blue area to move forward.

04 · Approach

From August to November 2024, through to launch in January

Discovery ran into a classic obstacle with this kind of profile: few target users were available for interviews. So the approach had to adapt: build hypotheses from field feedback, then validate them directly with beta testers rather than through traditional user research.

August 2024 · Discovery and hypotheses

Building hypotheses without available users

Few users from the target profile (CEOs, executives) were available for interviews. I built hypotheses about usage (mobility, speed, simplicity) from field feedback shared by sales and CSMs.

September – October 2024 · Design and validation

Validating hypotheses with beta testers

Hypotheses were validated directly with beta testers, in the absence of a large enough panel of target users. I iterated on the mobile signing flow in continuous collaboration with the product, sales, dev and CSM teams.

November 2024 · Final adjustments

Fine-tuning before release

A few changes based on beta tester feedback before shipping the final version. Launched in January 2025.

Leadership

Working hand in hand with the field

From the initial signal raised by sales and CSMs all the way to launch, I worked with the product, sales, dev and CSM teams in continuous collaboration, rather than a sequential hand-off between teams.

With a limited panel of target users, this closeness to the field was the main lever for building and challenging the design hypotheses.

05 · Results

Very positive customer feedback from the first demo

After the app was presented to one of our customers, the response was immediate: they especially appreciated the usability, the ease of use and the innovative nature of the app, which they had never seen anywhere else.

“It looks like a pleasure to navigate, great progress on usability and intuitiveness.” “A unique innovation that points to a bright future for Oodrive Sign.”

Customer feedback, myOodriveSign presentation
What I'd do differently

Discovery lacked target users available for interviews: decisions relied mostly on hypotheses, which were then validated with beta testers rather than before design began.
