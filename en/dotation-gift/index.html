Case study · Swile

A new digital gift offer securing a €33.1M switch

How to secure the switch of €33.1M in issuance value from former Bimpli customers to the 100% digital Swile Gift offer, while reassuring customers used to paper gift vouchers.

Client
Swile
Role
Product designer
Duration
4 months
Status
Shipped
01 · Context

An ordering flow to improve, and long-standing customers used to paper

The project was part of Swile's goal to improve the ordering flow for its customers' gift vouchers. The existing ordering flow had usability issues that needed fixing.

At the same time, former Bimpli customers were about to move to the new platform and the Swile offer: used to paper gift vouchers, they now had to adapt to a 100% digital offer.

Key takeaway: €33.1M in issuance value to move safely from one offer to the other.

02 · Problem

How do you reassure without degrading the experience?

How could we reassure long-standing customers while improving the flow, without the move to all-digital feeling like a loss?

  • An existing ordering flow with known usability issues
  • Bimpli customers used to paper, moving to an offer designed to be 100% digital
  • High financial stakes: €33.1M in issuance value to secure during the switch
03 · Solution

A non-personalized paper flyer, a bridge to the Swile app

The chosen solution combines the familiar feel of paper with the simplicity of digital: a new way to distribute the gift allowance, as a non-personalized flyer with a QR code that takes end users straight to their gift in the Swile app.

  • Non-personalized paper flyer that can be handed out at events such as a company Christmas party
  • QR code that leads straight to the gift allowance in the Swile app
  • A new onboarding and improved order tracking to reassure users discovering digital
04 · Approach

4 months, from scoping to production

I partnered with a product manager during scoping, and we brought in the tech lead early to anticipate technical constraints.

Phase 1 · Scoping

Frame the problem before designing

Built a Lean Canvas and ran a usability audit of the existing flow against Bastien & Scapin's criteria, paired with a product manager and with the tech lead involved from this phase on.

Phase 2 · Design

Map out, then design the new flow

Workshops to define use cases and the happy-path user flow, a service blueprint mapping every front-end, back-end and support interaction, a focus group with the sales teams (5 Gift sales reps, 1 CSM) to test appetite for the flyer, then ideation and a first version of the ordering flow.

Phase 3 · Testing and production

Validate with customers before slicing the work

Usability tests with 9 customers and a readout, analysis of the results and iterations, a new onboarding and improved order tracking, then slicing the solution into releases and shipping to production.

Leadership

Scope as a pair, bring tech in from day one

Rather than designing alone and discovering technical constraints downstream, I partnered with a product manager from scoping onward and brought in the tech lead early, so we could address technical constraints before they blocked the design.

That close collaboration extended to the sales teams, through a focus group dedicated to testing appetite for the flyer before we even designed the first flow.

05 · Results

A paper format that exceeded expectations

Usability tests (9 customers, 7 Swile admins and 2 Bimpli admins, run on Maze) confirmed the appetite for paper: 100% of testers successfully ordered the flyer, and Bimpli admins showed real interest in ordering it. That's a strong signal from the very group we expected to be most resistant to change.

What I'd do differently

The need to personalize messages (employee's first or last name) only surfaced during user testing, when it could have been anticipated in the focus group with the sales teams, before designing the first flow.
