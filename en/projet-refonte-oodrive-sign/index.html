Case study · 2024 – 2025

Oodrive Sign redesign simplifying e-signature

How to make an e-signature platform, seen as complex and unpleasant to use, genuinely fit its users' needs. From the initial audit to the production release, through three rounds of user testing.

Client
Oodrive Sign
Role
Lead designer
Duration
11 months
Status
Release 1 in production
01 · Context

A platform seen as complex and unpleasant to use

The project was part of a broader effort to improve Oodrive Sign, the company's e-signature platform. The product hadn't been driven by a structured product approach for several years: users found it complex and visually unappealing, and no in-depth work had been done to fix that.

Key takeaway: 85% of users only needed a simple flow, yet everyone went through a journey designed for complex cases.

02 · Problem

Navigation and flows that needed a ground-up rethink

Beyond the general feeling of complexity, the challenge was twofold: make the interface consistent with the rest of the Oodrive products, and simplify signing flows that had become far too heavy for how most users actually used them.

  • A home page that offered users no real value
  • A single signing flow, designed for complex cases, forced on 100% of users, including the 85% who only needed a simple flow
  • No structured product approach for several years, which had let pain points pile up without any trade-offs being made
03 · Solution

A visual redesign, rethought navigation, a simpler flow

The chosen solution combines a visual redesign aligned with the rest of the Oodrive products and a new navigation, with a deliberate simplification of the main flows rather than a mere cosmetic facelift.

  • A new home page, with a welcome message, a clear view of the user's scope and shortcut widgets to save users time
  • A new 1-click signing flow, built for 85% of users and shipped in several releases
  • A new bulk signing flow

04 · Approach

11 months, from the initial audit to the production release

I worked closely with all stakeholders (PM, PO, sales teams, CSMs, data, front-end and back-end developers) at every key stage of the process, from the initial audit to the production release.

Q4 2024 · Discovery and scoping

Understand, scope, prototype

Usability audit based on Nielsen's 10 heuristics, qualitative interviews with CSMs and sales reps, design sprint (brainstorming, user journey map, How Might We…), a nominal use case written from the main user stories, Crazy 8 workshop, Business Model Canvas. First UX/UI design phase, interactive prototype, user testing on the nominal use case (SUS score 85/100), first iteration, prioritization and 2025 roadmap.

Q1 2025 · Deep dive and development

Iterate on the main flow

Second round of user testing on the main flow (SUS score 87.25/100), new iterations based on feedback, regular exchanges with sales and CSM teams, two new features added from field needs, prioritization and release planning for Q2.

Q2 2025 · Progressive rollout

Fine-tune and ship to production

Third round of user testing (SUS score 89/100), final adjustments and iterations, prioritization of Q3 development, release 1 shipped to production.

Leadership

Bringing in a user-centered product culture

Oodrive Sign hadn't been driven by a structured product approach for several years. Beyond the design work itself, I introduced and spread a user-centered design culture, through hands-on education with internal teams.

In practice, this meant ongoing collaboration with all stakeholders (PM, PO, sales, CSMs, data, front-end and back-end developers) rather than design work in isolation.

05 · Results

A usability score that kept climbing

Each testing round led to meaningful iterations, with one main goal: secure the flows for existing customers and cut the jargon for prospects and new customers unfamiliar with the product.

What I'd do differently

Part of the project's time went into building a product culture internally. I would have set up shorter, more frequent testing cycles earlier, to iterate faster from the start.
