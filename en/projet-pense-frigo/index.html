Pense-Frigo good to the last bite

An anti-waste app that remembers what is in your fridge. Scan your till receipt and it tells you what to eat first.

01 · Context

I bought what I already had and threw away what I forgot

Food waste came from two places: products forgotten at the back of the fridge, and opened products when you no longer remember when they were opened. Inventory apps exist, but people drop them quickly because logging groceries takes too long.

The project was first called “Frigo sans ouvrir”, knowing what is inside without opening the door. It became Pense-Frigo once the promise got clearer: finishing what you bought.

Key takeaway: the real barrier is not knowing what expires, it is the time it takes to fill in the app.

02 · Positioning

Two promises competitors don't keep

One-gesture logging, from a till receipt or a photo of the fridge, and tracking of opened products. The rest of the roadmap serves these two promises.

03 · Iterations

Three styles in a few days

The first version shipped on 15 October. In five days and 32 versions, the main screen changed three times, each time to put what needs eating first further forward.

04 · Scanning

Filling your fridge in under a minute

You take a photo of your till receipt, or of the inside of your fridge. Claude reads the lines, matches the products and estimates the dates. Nothing is added without a review screen, because receipt labels are abbreviated and vary from one store to another.

  • A small server keeps the API key, and the app stores everything on the phone.
  • Scanning uses the most accurate model, recipes a model about 40 times cheaper: a design decision as much as a cost one.
05 · Research

Three hypotheses to confirm before going further

  • Logging is the barrier: people drop inventory apps because it takes too long.
  • Opened products cause the most waste, more than products never opened.
  • The fridge weighs on one person in the household, and it feels like a mental load.

To test them, I prepared an interview guide (ten 30-minute interviews with working couples and flatmates aged 25 to 40) and a 20-question survey to measure at a larger scale. In the app, a “Give feedback” page and anonymous analytics track how the first testers actually use it.

06 · Roadmap

Each version has an exit criterion

V1 · in progress

Prove the differentiation

Receipt and fridge photo scanning, an “I opened it” button and remaining quantity, use-by versus best-before with a reassuring tone. V2 starts when most testers add their groceries by receipt or photo rather than by hand.

V2

Zero effort, every day

A weekly review by swiping products, an automatic shopping list, at most one notification a day. V3 starts when users come back every week without a reminder.

V3

The household

A fridge shared between household members, sync, and a yoghurt mascot that never makes you feel guilty. Sharing is planned in the data model from V1.

07 · What I learned

Treating a side project like a real product

  • Success is measured by how easy logging is, not by the number of features.
  • With AI, reviewing is part of the experience: the user always stays in control.
  • Coding it myself forces me to trade off cost, reliability and comfort, like a product team.
